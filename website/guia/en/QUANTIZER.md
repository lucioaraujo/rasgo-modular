## O que é
QUANTIZER pins a continuous signal to the notes of a scale. A value that rises and falls freely, coming from a TURING, an SH or a CHAOS, comes out as a sequence of in-tune notes. In most patches, it is what turns chance into melody.

## Como pensar nele
Control signals vary continuously, and musical notes don't. Without a quantizer, a melody generated by chance falls between the notes and sounds out of tune. QUANTIZER rounds each value to the nearest note of the chosen scale. With a pulse on the TRIG input, it only changes note on the pulse, and the melody gains a rhythm. The GATE output pulses whenever the note actually changes, which is handy for firing an envelope only on new notes. The ROOT and SCL inputs change the tonic and the scale by cable: connected to the outputs of HARMONY, the melody's key changes as the music goes on.

## Controles
- SCALE: the scale, out of thirteen: chromatic, major, natural minor, Dorian, Phrygian dominant, Lydian, melodic minor, minor pentatonic, major pentatonic, hirajoshi, whole tone, octaves only, and the sieve.
- ROOT: the tonic, the scale's starting note, from C to B.
- RANGE: how many octaves the input signal covers, from 1 to 6. More octaves, bigger leaps.
- GLIDE: makes the pitch slide from one note to the next.
- HYST: a tolerance margin. With an unsteady signal, the note doesn't keep flipping between two neighbors.
- AFIN: the tuning, applied after the note is chosen: 12-tone equal temperament (as before), just, Pythagorean, meantone, 19 or 31 divisions of the octave. The three historical tunings are measured from the tonic and follow it when it changes.
- M1, R1, M2, R2: Xenakis's sieve, used by scale number 13. A note passes if its remainder when divided by M1 is R1, or if its remainder when divided by M2 is R2. With an M that does not divide 12, the scale does not repeat at the octave.
- AND: turns the sieve's or into and: a note has to pass both sieves. If no note passes, the sieve falls back to or, so it never goes silent.
Inputs: CV, the signal to quantize; TRSP, to transpose before quantizing; TRIG, the pulse that decides when to change note; ROOT and SCL, which, when patched, take over the tonic and the scale from the knobs. Outputs: PTCH, the pitch; GATE, a pulse on each new note; ST, the note in semitones.

## Experimente
1. Connect the CV output of a TURING to QUANTIZER's CV input, and the CLK output of CLOCK to the TURING's CLK input.
2. Connect PTCH to the 1V/O of an OSC, and the OSC's SIN output to an ENVELOPE connected to the MIXER. Connect QUANTIZER's GATE to the envelope's GATE.
3. Change SCALE and hear the same sequence of values become another melody. Change ROOT to transpose.
